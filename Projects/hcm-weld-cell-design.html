PROJECT 04 · MECHANICAL DESIGN

HCM Equipment and Robot Tooling Design

I was the mechanical design lead for this project, responsible for the overall mechanical design of the equipment and its integration into the existing cell. My responsibilities included creating, reviewing, and updating detailed CAD models, drawings, BOMs, and assembly documentation to ensure that the equipment could be manufactured, installed, and operated reliably and produce quality products for HCM.

01 / Project overview / background

Project Overview

This project involved upgrading manufacturing equipment to support production of both a new and current model unit. The project included the design and integration of material-handling jigs as robot EOATs, quality-control tooling, part-staging equipment, and robotic equipment. The project required several interconnected systems to operate safely and reliably around associates, a turntable, existing tooling, new robot bases, and equipment supplied by a global line builder. My responsibilities covered design development, drawing and BOM detailing, assembly validation, interference checking, robot reach and motion simulation,installation support, and redesign work. This was one of the largest and most challenging projects I worked on during my internship. I joined the project after the original design reviews had been completed, meaning the design was considered complete. However, detailed assembly reviews and installation activities revealed several issues that required redesign and technical decision-making before the equipment could be released and installed.

03 / Material Handling Jigs

EOAT Jig for Robots

These handling jigs were designed to support the robots in picking up and placing parts during the process and transefering quality and location from each part of the process from the pre set jig, to sealer application to turn table, to being held while getting welded. The jigs needed to be robust enough to hold the parts securely while also allowing the robots to access and manipulate them efficiently.

04 / Weld equipment

Weld robot

I worked with existing weld heads and validated their fit and integration with the new robot models. This included checking mounting interfaces, tool clearances, surrounding equipment, and the available access required for the weld heads to perform their intended operations.

I performed robot simulations to verify reach and access throughout the required weld paths using the new robot model. These simulations helped identify potential interference conditions and confirm that the robot could reach the required positions while maintaining a practical and serviceable cell arrangement.

Payload calculations were completed to verify that the new robots could safely support the weld heads and associated tooling. I also performed FEA on the newly designed robot bases to validate their structural performance under the expected loading conditions and support the design decisions with engineering analysis.

05 / Part positioning

Pre-Set Quality Jig

The design required new quick-change tooling and templates to be integrated with an existing frame and quick-change bases.

The scope included:

  • Designing new quick-change tooling
  • Creating matching templates
  • Integrating the tooling with the existing pre-set jig frame
  • Adding an additional quick-change base to the structure
  • Reviewing access, clearance, and repeatability
  • Checking the capacity of the existing support system after the redesign

The most significant technical challenge of the project because an installation concern was identified immediately before the equipment was scheduled to be installed.

Redesign Approach

The original design mounted the new assembly to an existing guarding structure. Although this design had passed the formal review process, the quality team raised concerns that the guarding frame might not provide enough rigidity to maintain repeatable template positioning during production.

To address this concern, I developed an alternative mounting strategy that located the assembly directly from existing machined surfaces and jig structure. This approach reduced reliance on the guarding and used more controlled datum features to improve repeatability and reduce the risk of movement caused by deflection.

The redesign required me to:

  • Create and modify multiple 3D models
  • Update detailed manufacturing drawings
  • Review the surrounding assembly interfaces
  • Check for new interference conditions
  • Assess the effect of the added structure on the overall jig
  • Verify that the revised design could be manufactured and installed within the project timeline

Load and Centre-of-Gravity Analysis

The additional weldment and quick-change tooling increased the overall weight of the pre-set jig assembly. I completed load and centre-of-gravity calculations to determine whether the existing support cylinder had sufficient capacity.

  • The added mass of the new structure and tooling
  • The revised centre of gravity
  • The resulting moment and loading conditions
  • Existing cylinder holding capacity
  • Whether additional counterweight or support would be required
  • The ability of the system to operate safely and reliably

This analysis supported the design decision with engineering calculations rather than assumptions and demonstrated the importance of reassessing existing equipment when new components are added.

Installation Validation and Gemba-Based Design

A further issue was discovered during installation when the actual guarding conditions were compared with the CAD design. The original design assumed that the left- and right-hand assemblies were mirrored. Although the primary jig structures were mirrored, the surrounding guarding frames were not identical.

As a result, the new quick-change assembly interfered with the guarding on one side of the equipment. Resolving the issue required modifications to the quick-change mounting weldment, Lexan guarding, supporting frame components, and the interface between the pre-set jig and surrounding equipment.

This experience reinforced the importance of validating designs at Gemba, the actual installation location. A design can appear correct in CAD and still require adjustment when real equipment, access conditions, manufacturing variation, and surrounding interfaces are considered together.

06 / Part Staging Rack

Part-Staging Rack

The part-staging rack was designed to hold and present the new model parts that associates load onto the turntable. The rack needed to support the parts securely while also allowing efficient manual loading and unloading.

The design considerations included:

  • Part stability and support
  • Associate access and ergonomics
  • Correct part orientation
  • Clear separation between staged parts
  • Compatibility with the turntable and surrounding equipment
  • Structural stability during normal use
  • Ease of fabrication and installation
