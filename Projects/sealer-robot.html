PROJECT 03 · MECHANICAL DESIGN

Sealer Robot

Mechanical design lead for an automated sealing system completed at Honda of Canada Mfg.

Explore the project ↓
RoleMechanical design lead
Completed atHonda of Canada Mfg.
EnvironmentCATIA V5 / DELMIA V5
01 / Project overview / background

Integrate an additional ceiling-mounted robot into an existing cell to support production.

The new robot was designed to take over a sealer path from an existing robot, helping the cell meet the required cycle time while maintaining production quality. This project provided my first opportunity to experience the complete mechanical design process

02 / Ownership

My role

I was the mechanical design lead for this project, responsible for the overall mechanical design of the sealer robot and its integration into the existing cell. My responsibilities included creating detailed CAD models, detailed drawing packages, performing interference checks, and ensuring that the design met all functional and safety requirements. I collaborated closely with the automation team to ensure seamless integration of the mechanical components with the control systems.

My responsibilities included:

  • Designing and integrating a second door keeper to hold the vehicle door open as the robot entered the vehicle.
  • Designing the components for the robot's end-of-arm tooling and dress-out.
  • Integrating the robot into the existing pneumatic and sealer circuits.
  • Developing and modifying 3D models using CATIA V5.
  • Creating manufacturing drawings and releasing design revisions.
  • Preparing a design specification sheet.
  • Leading design reviews and presenting project information to stakeholders.
  • Supporting the installation and production startup phases.
  • Performing FEA validation of the ceiling-mounted robot support structure.
Technical toolkit
  • CATIA V5 and DELMIA V5
  • Robot reach and motion simulation studies
  • Interference and clearance checks
  • Cell layout and equipment installation support
  • Detailed drawing packages
  • Engineering design reviews
03 / Reflection

One of the main challenges was managing undocumented changes to existing equipment. Much of the equipment in the cell had been installed approximately ten years earlier and had been modified over time without corresponding updates to the drawings, models, or BOMs. As a result, the documented equipment did not always match the as-built condition. To resolve these discrepancies, I performed repeated Gemba checks and manually investigated the equipment in the cell. In some cases, I traced pneumatic circuits directly to understand how the system operated and determine how the new robot could be integrated. I identified missing components, determined their functions, created updated models, and released revised drawings to improve the accuracy of the engineering documentation. I also updated the end-of-arm tooling documentation to include accurate center-of-gravity calculations, teach-center-point locations, and mass properties that had not previously been documented.
