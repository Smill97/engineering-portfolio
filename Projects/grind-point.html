01 / Project overview / background

Automating a critical vehicle grounding-point process

The PA2 Grind Point Robot Automation Project was the largest and most technically challenging project I worked on during my internship. It was a $1.8 million investment focused on automating a manual grinding process used to create vehicle grounding points.

Associates previously used a hand drill with an abrasive pad to remove paint from four bolt-hole locations, exposing the steel beneath. These locations serve as grounding points for the vehicle's wiring harness. Because the work required associates to reach into the vehicle and perform the process in difficult-to-access areas, it was ergonomically demanding and challenging to complete consistently.

The project introduced new automation technology to improve process consistency, associate ergonomics, and quality verification. A robot performs the grinding operation repetitively, while a vision system confirms that the required material has been removed. This verification is especially important because the grounding points are critical to the vehicle's electrical functionality and overall quality.

02 / Ownership

My role

As the Mechanical Design Lead, I was responsible for designing the mechanical equipment and systems within the robot cell, producing detailed 2D and 3D drawing packages, and developing BOMs.

My responsibilities included:

  • Body-positioning equipment
  • Inspection check stand
  • Vision-system stand
  • Door-keeper assemblies
  • Media-staging equipment
  • End-of-arm tooling (EOAT)
  • Automatic tool changer
  • Pneumatic circuits and component selection
  • Robot dress-out
  • Installation coordination and design reviews

To support the design and ensure that the systems met project requirements, I developed design quality-in-progress sheets, design specification sheets, DFMEAs, engineering calculations, and supporting technical documentation. I also managed my project schedule to ensure that design and installation milestones were met.

03 / Engineering analysis

Engineering Analysis and Design Validation

A major part of my role involved validating the performance, safety, and reliability of the mechanical systems. I completed robot payload calculations to confirm that the grinder, vision system, brackets, sensors, dress-out, hoses, and cables remained within the robot's allowable payload limits.

I also performed FEA on the end-of-arm tooling to evaluate its rigidity and structural performance. The analysis helped verify that the tooling would not experience excessive deformation, fatigue failure, or unacceptable deflection while the robot was moving and applying grinding forces.

Additional calculations included:

  • Robot transfer-unit speed calculations to verify that the process could meet the required cycle time
  • Cylinder force and moment calculations
  • Automatic tool-changer repeatability calculations
  • Spring-force calculations for the tool changer
  • Pneumatic-circuit design and component selection

In addition to the technical design work, I presented mechanical concepts and analysis results during design reviews, communicated technical information to project stakeholders, and coordinated installation activities.

04 / Project significance

The Grind Point project challenged me to apply and expand the technical skills I developed throughout my internship. It was also the project where I began to demonstrate my ability to take ownership of a complex design effort and step into a leadership role.

Through this experience, I strengthened my skills in mechanical design, engineering analysis, automation, technical communication, project coordination, and design validation.
